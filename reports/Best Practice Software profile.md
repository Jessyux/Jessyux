# Best Practice dominates GP software, cloud lags

Best Practice Software (Bp) is the incumbent in Australian general practice software. It is a founder-controlled company: the Pyefinch family owns 51% and Sonic Healthcare 49%. Reported FY2025 revenue was **A$50.07m** and staff number roughly 200. The company says Bp Premier is used by **7,000+ practices and 27,000+ clinicians**, and independent estimates put its share of Australian GP practices at about **60% to 80%**. For a product manager, the central fact is that this lead rests on switching costs, not on a modern product. Bp Premier is still an on-premise SQL Server desktop application. The cloud product, Bp Omni, has not reached Australian GPs three years after its 2023 NZ beta. In May 2025 the company replaced "move to Omni" with an opt-in, incremental "Journey to the Cloud." Since 2024 Bp has built its strategy around partners and minority stakes rather than acquisitions: Halo Connect as the mandatory integration layer, Lyrebird AI scribing (free to all Premier customers since February 2026) and a lead investment in clinical-AI firm Medcast in September 2026. A PM joining now would face five pressures at once. Cloud-native challengers (Telstra Health's MedicalDirector Helix, MediRecords, Halaxy) are pushing. Standalone AI scribes such as Heidi could pull clinicians' workflows outside the record. A July 2026 price rise hit part-time GPs hardest. Release-quality problems keep recurring. And regulatory mandates arrive faster than a legacy codebase can absorb them cheaply. Several premises worth checking before an interview are not supported by the sources: no private-equity owner (for example Pacific Equity Partners), no Dedalus link, and no Clinic to Cloud or Medical Wizard acquisition was found.

## Founder-controlled, Sonic-backed, about A$50m in revenue

Bp was founded in Bundaberg, Queensland in **2004** by GP Dr Frank Pyefinch. In 2002 he had exited MedicalDirector, the business that is now Bp's main rival ([History & Values](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/about/history-values/); [Health Services Daily, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)). Ownership is **Pyefinch family 51%, Sonic Healthcare 49%**, reported consistently in June 2024 and September 2026 ([Medical Republic, 20 Jun 2024](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/why-does-sonic-want-to-control-both-cubiko-and-best-practice/108495); [Dealroom, 21 Sep 2026](https://dealroom.co/news/155154-best-practice-leads-3-9m-raise-for-australian-clinical-ai-startup-medcas/)). In 2024 Medical Republic reported several further details. Sonic does not control Bp. Sonic CFO Christopher Wilks is one of only two directors. Lorraine Pyefinch holds the family's shares. And an unnamed "large corporate" made a high offer some years earlier that never completed. The same article is internally inconsistent about the control margin ([Medical Republic, 20 Jun 2024](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/why-does-sonic-want-to-control-both-cubiko-and-best-practice/108495)). The date and price of Sonic's stake were not found. The private-equity churn in this market belongs to MedicalDirector, which passed from Affinity Equity Partners (A$155m, 2016) to Telstra Health (A$340m) ([Health Services Daily, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)).

**FY2025 consolidated revenue was A$50.07m.** This figure has a single source, and the underlying filing was not viewed ([Health Services Daily, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)). Dividing that revenue across about 7,000 practices gives roughly A$7k per practice per year. That is an analyst's back-of-envelope figure, not a sourced one.

An undated Bundaberg Now item says Hodges once left for Queensland Health ([Bundaberg Now](https://www.bundabergnow.com/?p=14822)). It most likely describes an earlier departure, because he is quoted as CEO in September 2026.

For a product candidate, the relevant chain is on the October 2026 Leadership page. There is **no titled CPO**. **CTO Jessica White** leads "product, platform, partnerships and technology". She joined Bp in 2015. Reporting into that area are **Simon Williams** (Chief of Product Technology) and **Joel Mortensen** (Director, Product Design), who covers roadmaps, the product design lab and sector analysis. ## Bp Premier carries the business while cloud slips

**Bp Premier** is the revenue base. It ships named releases: Orchid SP2 (May 2024, which added the Lyrebird scribe), Spectra (October 2024, with an improved scribe and eScripts) and the current **Oxford 1.14.0.1114, released 9 June 2026**. Oxford SP1 was still in industry preview in July 2026 ([Pulse+IT, Orchid SP2](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-releases-big-update-to-orchid-including-lyrebird-scribe-integration/); [Pulse+IT, Spectra](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-releases-spectra-version-featuring-improved-ai-scribe-and-escripts-capability/); [Oxford release notes](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/oxford/WhatsNew/BpPremier_Oxford_ReleaseNotes.htm)). Much of Oxford is compliance work: immunisation rules, MyMedicare and claiming changes, a 12-month bulk-billing voucher window, and **digital SMS Assignment of Benefits from 1 July 2026** ([Bp blog, 21 May 2026](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/blog/whats-new-in-bp-premier-oxford/)).

Patient engagement sits in a set of add-ons around Premier. **Bp SMS/Comms** handles reminders and recalls ([Bp SMS](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/products/bp-sms)). The **Best Health** patient app dates from 2019 ([Bp](https://bpsoftware.net/best-health-app-overview)). **Best Health Booking** launched on 25 March 2024 and shows patients the Premier appointment book in real time ([Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-launches-best-health-booking-online-booking-management-solution/)). **Payments** run through partners, not a Bp product. Tyro is the "first and longest-standing" integration, and the Bp Payments Utility also supports HICAPS and CommBank Smart Health ([Bp Tyro page](https://bpsoftware.net/partner-tyro); [Bp KB](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/bppremier/spectra/PaymentIntegrations/PaymentIntegrations.htm)). "Digital payments" was promised as a 2025 cloud module, but no evidence was found that it shipped.

Cloud is where Bp is furthest behind its own earlier plans. **Bp Omni** started as "Project Titanium" around 2017. It reached beta only in **June 2023**, for NZ allied health, and Medical Republic's own *estimate* of an Australian release by 2025 did not come true ([Pulse+IT, 2017](https://www.pulseit.news/new-zealand-digital-health/best-practice-eyes-movement-in-the-new-zealand-pms-market/); [Medical Republic, 3 Jul 2023](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/best-practice-hints-at-a-transformational-plan/17474)). On **8 May 2025** Bp announced **"Journey to the Cloud"** ([Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-software-announces-new-journey-to-the-cloud/)). It is an opt-in path promising "No forced migrations, no loss of control". The planned sequence is Best Practice Mobile first, then cloud modules (smart forms, templates, digital payments), then BpID single sign-on, then cloud hosting and linked practices, and only after that a move toward Omni, on a 2025–2028 timeline ([Bp, 24 Jun 2025](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/?p=146536)). The mobile app was in FHIR beta in May 2024 ([Pulse+IT, 13 May 2024](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-on-fhir-with-mobile-app-for-gps-on-the-go/)). Its vendor-page launch date has since moved from 2025 to "coming 2026", and no adoption numbers exist. By October 2026 the Bp Omni URL redirects to a general product page that no longer mentions Omni ([bestpracticesoftware.com/bp-omni/](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/bp-omni/)). That points to Omni being de-emphasised in favour of cloud-enabling Premier step by step, though this is an inference and Bp has said nothing about it. In September 2026 a Bp executive, attributed to the CTO, was quoted conceding "We have on-premise product that will change soon" ([Health Services Daily, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)).

In NZ, Bp entered by buying Houston Medical's VIP.net in **April 2015**. **Bp VIP.net goes into maintenance mode on 31 December 2026**, with support ending 30 June 2027 ([Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-buys-nz-software-vendor-houston-medical/); [Bp sunset notice](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/bp-vip-net-sunset-nz/)). That forces those NZ customers to migrate somewhere, and the destination could be Omni or a competitor. Specialist rival Xestro has already written publicly about the sunset ([Xestro, 30 Mar 2026](https://xestro.com/2026/03/30/from-our-ceo-the-end-of-an-era-farewell-to-bp-vip-net/)). Bp's own materials mention "two additional software companies" acquired. Only Houston Medical is named, and no acquisition since 2016 was found ([History & Values](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/about/history-values/)). A Bp Allied product existed historically, but its current status is unknown ([Pulse+IT, older](https://pulseitmagazine.com.au/allied-health/3924-best-practice-releases-bp-allied-v5-with-medicare-online-tyro-integration/)).

## Partners and minority stakes replace acquisitions

Since 2024 Bp has grown its ecosystem rather than its portfolio. **Halo Connect** is a FHIR integration platform built by the Cubiko developers, and Bp holds a stake in its operator, Halo Wing. It became the **mandatory integration route for Bp Premier from 31 December 2025** ([The PHN](https://thephn.com.au/news/halo-connect-and-best-practice-action-required-before-31-december-2025); [Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/anz/cubiko-developers-launch-cloud-based-interoperability-platform-halo-connect)). Bp earns substantial revenue from partners that pay for access through it ([Health Services Daily, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)). Partner counts conflict: "over 130" on one Bp page, "200+" on the Premier page, and about 250 according to Health Services Daily ([Bp](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/bp-omni/); [Bp Premier](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/products/bp-premier/)). The difference probably reflects curated "Partner Network" members versus all Halo-connected integrations.

AI follows the same partner model. Bp announced a Lyrebird scribe partnership on **6 February 2024**, invested **A$3.378m** in Lyrebird, and on **16 February 2026** gave every Bp Premier customer a free Lyrebird tier ([Bp blog, Feb 2024](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/blog/best-practice-and-lyrebird-health-partnership/); [Bp blog, Feb 2026](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/blog/lyrebird-free-now-available-for-bp-premier-customers/); [Health Services Daily](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)). On **21 September 2026** Bp led a raise in Medcast to scale MedLuma, an onshore clinical-knowledge AI tool that is to be integrated into Bp software. Medcast had been a Bp CPD partner since August 2025. **The raise amount conflicts.** Dealroom reports **US$3.49m (about A$3.9m)** and Pulse+IT reports **$5.4m** ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/155154-best-practice-leads-3-9m-raise-for-australian-clinical-ai-startup-medcas/); [Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-leads-5-4m-investment-in-clinical-ai/)). Hodges framed the deal by saying AI "will always complement – not replace – the clinical judgment" ([Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-leads-5-4m-investment-in-clinical-ai/)). Other dated integrations include Cubiko Calculate (May 2025), BESTMED aged-care medication (August 2025) and the HotHealth digital front door (December 2025) ([Pulse+IT, Cubiko](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/cubiko-launches-calculate-via-best-practice/); [Pulse+IT, BESTMED](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/bestmed-integrates-with-best-practice-on-residential-aged-care/); [Pulse+IT, HotHealth](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/connected-care-advances-with-hothealth-integration/)). Sonic also owns a majority of Cubiko, which ties Bp, Halo and Cubiko together through a common shareholder. In 2024 that link drew questions about Sonic's intentions ([Medical Republic, 20 Jun 2024](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/why-does-sonic-want-to-control-both-cubiko-and-best-practice/108495)).

## Retention depends on switching costs, which the challengers are attacking

### Competitors

The competitive threat comes from three directions. **Telstra Health's MedicalDirector** added Smart Scribe in February 2025, embedded Consultmed eReferrals in July 2025 and launched Smart Connect pathology eRequesting with Healius in March 2026. It markets **Helix** as server-free, and it is reportedly building a fully cloud, AI-based platform ([MedicalDirector](https://www.medicaldirector.com/press/telstra-health-launches-ai-powered-smart-scribe-in-medical-director-clinical); [Telstra Health](https://www.telstrahealth.com/telstra-health-partners-with-consultmed/); [Hospital + Healthcare](https://www.hospitalhealth.com.au/content/technology/product/telstra-health-smart-connect-pathology-erequesting-tool-1076063497); [Helix](https://www.telstrahealth.com/products/helix/)). Cloud-native challengers market directly against Bp's server burden. **MediRecords** launched its Evolve AI suite in February 2025, and **Halaxy** offers free core software ([MediRecords](https://medirecords.com/tag/ai-patient-summary/); [Halaxy, 28 Apr 2026](https://blog.halaxy.com/best-practice-vs-medical-director-vs-halaxy-why-australian-gps-are-choosing-a-new-foundation-for-their-practice/)). In the specialist market, **Magentus** (Genie, Gentu) hired ex-Xero executive Rachael Powell as CEO in July 2024 ([Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/tag/magentus/page/3/)). **Standalone AI scribes** such as Heidi integrate with Premier but reportedly have only shallow access to the record, and commentators warn of "shadow IT" scribes fragmenting clinical data ([Heidi](https://www.heidihealth.com/en-au/integrations/best-practice); [Health Services Daily](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)). The competitor evidence is mixed, though. Reader comments say Helix fails to send referrals properly to Queensland public hospitals, so cloud alone does not win customers.

### Customer pain points

The documented pain points are concrete. From **1 July 2026** the full-time licence rose 5% to **A$1,489.41 a year**, while the part-time licence rose 26% to **A$893.65**, taking it from 50% to 60% of the full fee. Medical Republic noted that part-timers are more likely to be women. Bp said the change aligns pricing "with actual system usage" ([Medical Republic, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/best-practice-fee-hike-higher-for-part-timers/126739)). Release quality is a recurring problem. Spectra/SP1 had clinical notes not saving. After Oxford SP1, some sites lost server–client and HALO Link connections. And whole-site outages usually mean restarting SQL Server on the practice's own server ([Bp blog](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/blog/an-update-for-users-of-bp-premier-spectra-spectra-sp1/); [Bp KB known issues](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/bppremier/oxford/WhatsNew/AllKnownIssues.htm); [Bp KB](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/spectra/Troubleshooting/PremierExits.htm)). One GP calls customers' tolerance of a "good enough" product a kind of "Stockholm syndrome". Practice owners bear the cost and risk of migrating, while the GPs who would benefit do not ([Health Services Daily, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)). **Unverified:** a single dev.to post claims CVE-2026-114119 (21 October 2026) describes a hardcoded SQL Server administrator password in Premier builds before 1.14.1.1128. Check the CVE record before repeating it ([dev.to](https://dev.to/kozhevniko/a-password-you-cannot-change-cve-2026-114119-and-the-hardcoded-sql-server-administrator-in-bp-2037)). No NPS, churn data or independent review base exists. SoftwareReview lists no written reviews of Bp Premier ([SoftwareReview](https://softwarereview.com/s/bp-premier)).

### Regulation

Regulation adds a steady stream of required work. **Share by Default** has required pathology and imaging reports to be uploaded to My Health Record since **1 July 2026**, and uploads more than doubled to over 5 million a week ([Dept of Health](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/modernising-my-health-record-improved-access-to-health-information); [ADHA, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.digitalhealth.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australians-already-seeing-the-benefits-of-sharing-health-information-by-default-08-07-2026.pdf)). eRequesting in Premier needs Lava SP3 or later with HPI-O and HPI-I identifiers recorded ([Brisbane South PHN](https://bsphn.org.au/documents/erequesting-fact-sheet.pdf)). Bp's Assignment of Benefit build reportedly cost about **A$1m, against about A$50k at MediRecords**. That figure comes from the competitor-affiliated column ([Health Services Daily](https://www.healthservicesdaily.com.au/is-the-dominance-of-best-practice-a-good-or-a-bad-thing/52729)). The 2024 MediSecure breach, which exposed data on about 12.9 million Australians, raised security expectations for prescribing software ([iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/medisecure-data-breach-affects-about-129-million-australians-609924)).

## Conclusion

Bp's position is two things at once. Its scale lets it absorb mandates that smaller vendors cannot, and it lets it charge partners for access through Halo Connect. But the same legacy architecture makes every mandate, release and cloud step expensive and risky. The 2025 reset from "migrate to Omni" to "cloud-enable Premier" is a bet that continuity keeps the installed base in place long enough for incremental modules to close the gap with server-free rivals. The free Lyrebird tier and the Medcast stake show that Bp now sees the AI layer, more than the PMS itself, as where clinicians could defect.

For a PM candidate, the strongest angle is retention through trust, not new features. That means release reliability on a hybrid on-prem and cloud stack, adoption metrics for opt-in cloud modules (none are published, so whoever builds them will own the story), how fair pricing feels to part-time and female GPs, and keeping scribe and clinical-AI data inside the record. The NZ VIP.net sunset in December 2026 is the nearest live test of whether Bp can migrate customers without losing them, and it is a natural interview question.
