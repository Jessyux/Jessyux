# Backbone products prove systems thinking best

A Head of Product treats systems thinking as **scope of impact made visible**. The evidence they look for is a candidate who names every team and system a change touches, states the trade-off made between them, and reports an outcome that belongs to the whole business rather than to one screen. Product writers frame the skill as situational awareness and "seeing the whole". It matters most for platform and operational-backbone products, whose customers are other teams and whose value comes from leverage. Career ladders and job ads make it a seniority marker: GitLab widens PM scope from cross-functional to cross-departmental as level rises, and Atlassian's senior and group PM ads name systems thinking outright. An internal admin platform like Montu admin, which patient support, fulfilment, pharmacy ops, warehouse and finance all work in, is therefore not a weaker story than a consumer feature. Told well, it is the strongest available proof of the skill. The way to tell it is a hub-and-spoke context diagram, one scoped service blueprint, one second-order cascade with before/after numbers, and an explicit note of what was deliberately left unoptimised. Best Practice Software makes this directly relevant. Bp Premier is itself a backbone: one desktop record shared by reception, clinicians and practice managers, wired to Medicare, My Health Record, ePrescribing and immunisation registers, and feeding about 60 partner integrations through a newly mandatory gateway. Its 2026 friction points (the pulled Assignment of Benefit release, the forced Halo Connect migration, the database-credential hardening) are textbook failures that spread across a system. A PM who can show they have already managed a multi-actor backbone is speaking Bp's native problem.

## Leaders define the skill as seeing the whole, then intervening at the right lever

The working definitions converge. John Cutler describes systems thinking for PMs as "situational awareness", the ability to accurately perceive the elements in your environment. He argues the job is shifting from shipping a product to designing and maintaining a **"product system"** that behaves non-linearly, so small changes can have large effects ([ProductPlan](https://www.productplan.com/systems-thinking/); [Mind the Product](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/a-guide-to-systems-thinking-for-product-managers/)). Peter Senge's older definition, "a discipline of seeing the whole", still anchors the literature ([Mind the Product](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/a-guide-to-systems-thinking-for-product-managers/)). Will Larson makes it operational. He models work as stocks, flows and feedback loops, and he shows that a model's job is to find leverage: faster deploys achieve nothing without a backlog of ready commits ([lethain.com](https://lethain.com/systems-thinking/)).

Donella Meadows' hierarchy of **leverage points** gives a PM the most useful vocabulary. Parameters and numbers sit at the bottom, the weakest place to intervene. Information flows (#6), rules (#5) and goals (#3) sit much higher ([Donella Meadows Project](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/)). Most feature requests on an admin tool are bottom-rung parameter tweaks: a new field, a different default. The high-leverage work changes who sees bad data and when, or which rule decides who owns a hand-off. A candidate who can say "we stopped tweaking fields and changed the information flow" is showing the skill, not just claiming it.

"Ecosystem thinking" is the layer above. In service-design usage, an ecosystem map answers *who* is involved in a service, and a blueprint answers *what* is involved in delivering it ([Queensland Government service design playbook](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/service-design-and-delivery/design-public-services/digital-service-design-playbook/all-plays/service-ecosystem-map)). Ecosystem thinking maps the actors: internal teams, partners, regulators, end users, and the value and data passing between them. Systems thinking explains how that web behaves when one part changes.

For platform products the two merge. Marty Cagan calls platform definition "one of the most difficult – but highest leverage – types of product management" ([SVPG](https://www.svpg.com/platform-product-management/)), and SVPG now treats "platform services" as a distinct group-PM domain ([SVPG](https://www.svpg.com/the-gpm-role/)). Team Topologies defines a platform's purpose as **reducing the cognitive load of the teams that consume it**. It warns that platforms mandated rather than adopted on merit often *increase* that load ([Team Topologies](https://teamtopologies.com/platform-engineering)). Both definitions measure success by other teams' outcomes, which is itself a systems view.

## Hiring managers look for named dependents, explicit trade-offs and a whole-business outcome

No named Head of Product or CPO surfaced in this research with a published list of "systems thinking" interview signals. The evidence comes from career ladders, job ads and interview-prep material, so read it as how the skill is tested, not as any one employer's rubric. Even so, the pattern is consistent. GitLab's PM framework widens expected scope from "Cross functional work" at Senior to "Across Departments" at Director ([GitLab Handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product/product-management/product-cdf-competencies/)). Atlassian ads ask senior PMs to apply systems thinking "to simplify complexity", and ask group PMs to design for "interconnected product components" ([Built In](https://builtin.com/job/group-product-manager-jira/3738183); [Accel jobs](https://jobs.accel.com/companies/atlassian/jobs/54765854-principal-product-manager-confluence-flywheel)). Lenny Rachitsky's survey of PM interview loops shows the skill being tested indirectly. A Chime take-home asks which other product teams a plan would affect and how to address their concerns. An Airbnb exercise asks candidates to balance both sides of a marketplace ([Lenny's Newsletter, archived](https://personal-learnings.pages.dev/reading_room/issues/lennys_newsletter/0504_how-to-interview-product-managers.html)).

The signals that recur across these sources can be stated plainly. The candidate maps technical, organisational and user dependencies. They spot feedback loops and cascading effects. They anticipate unintended consequences. And they trade off **local optimisation against global outcomes** ([InterviewStack.io](https://www.interviewstack.io/product_manager/categories/question-bank/systems-thinking-and-interdependencies)). Strong answers name the trade-off, justify it, and say whether it can be reversed ([igotanoffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/system-design-questions-product-managers); [Uxcel](https://uxcel.com/lessons/navigating-trade-offs-and-dependencies-458)). Portfolio reviewers add one more test. Sachin Rekhi checks whether a candidate can separate the problem from their particular solution ([Sachin Rekhi](https://www.sachinrekhi.com/evaluating-a-product-managers-portfolio)). Coaching sources agree that a few deep case studies with real constraints beat many feature summaries ([Leland](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/product-manager-portfolio)).

The pitfalls are just as clear, and a Head of Product will probe for them. The first is the feature team that ships roadmap output without owning outcomes ([SVPG](https://www.svpg.com/product-vs-feature-teams/)). The second is siloed metrics: each department is measured on the touchpoints it owns, while customers experience the whole journey ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/)). The third is optimising a flow that is not the constraint ([lethain.com](https://lethain.com/systems-thinking/)). The fourth is the mandated platform that adds load instead of removing it ([platformengineering.org](https://platformengineering.org/talks-library/platform-as-a-product)). The fifth runs the other way: Cutler himself warns that systems overthinking can become an excuse not to ship ([yespress.io](https://yespress.io/john-cutler)). The best signal of judgement is a candidate who mapped the system *and still shipped a thin slice*. Team Topologies calls that the "Thinnest Viable Platform" ([Team Topologies](https://teamtopologies.com/platform-engineering)).

## Present Montu admin as a hub whose customers are five teams

An internal admin platform invites a weak telling: a list of screens and features for "the ops team". The stronger telling, built from platform-PM writing, treats each dependent function as a customer with its own job. Kateryna Korotieieva argues that internal teams deserve SLAs, versioning policies and feedback loops. She asks two cascade questions of every change: "What breaks if we delay it?" and "What does this unlock for others?". She also argues that classic KPIs miss enablement, so platform success should be read through ecosystem health: adoption, integration speed and reliability ([Mind the Product](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/the-platform-pm-building-an-ecosystem-not-just-a-product/)).

That suggests a five-beat arc, synthesised from the sources rather than taken from any canonical template. First, what the platform is and who depends on it: patient support, fulfilment, pharmacy ops, warehouse, finance. Second, the upstream systems feeding it and the data contracts with them. Third, one change and its cascade. Fourth, how the teams were aligned through rollout, training and SLAs. Fifth, an ecosystem metric paired with one whole-business outcome. Pairing a team-level metric with an enterprise-level one is what shows the jump from optimising one team to optimising the business, the same jump career ladders describe. The candidate's own before/after numbers will carry more weight than any industry statistic. Most published figures on internal-tool payoffs are vendor case studies. One example is WalkMe's claim of a 70%+ fall in help-desk tickets at Origin ([WalkMe](https://www.walkme.com/customer-stories/origin/)). Stripe's widely quoted finding, that about 42% of developer time goes on technical debt and maintenance, is a survey-based estimate whose definitions critics have questioned ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=541182); [CodeScene](https://codescene.com/hubfs/web_docs/Business-costs-of-technical-debt.pdf)). Calling such figures directional is itself a signal of rigour.

The cascade beat is where the story is won. NN/g notes that poor customer experiences often start in internal weaknesses such as data problems or wait times. It also notes that data captured once early in a journey can be reused downstream, avoiding inconsistency ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/)). For Montu admin, a credible chain starts with validation at the point of entry. That leads to fewer prescription or order data errors, then fewer manual corrections by pharmacy ops and fewer tickets for patient support. Fulfilment and warehouse work less rework, finance spends less effort reconciling, and the business sees a lower cost to serve and lower compliance risk. Conway's law, that organisations design systems mirroring their communication structures ([Conway, 1968](https://www.melconway.com/Home/Committees_Paper.html)), supplies the most memorable interview line if it fits the facts: the admin tool's fragmentation mirrored the org's silos, so part of the fix was a process or ownership change, not just UI. Finally, the candidate should say what they chose *not* to optimise for one team, because that sentence is where local-versus-global judgement becomes visible.

The visuals should run at two zoom levels. The headline is a C4-style system context or hub-and-spoke view. The platform sits at the centre, people and teams surround it, and upstream and downstream systems appear as boxes, with no technology detail, because C4 designs this view to be shown to non-technical audiences ([C4 model](https://c4model.com/diagrams/system-context)). Edges should be labelled with what flows (orders, prescriptions, payments), not with protocols. The second level is a service blueprint scoped to one scenario, such as script-to-dispatch. It runs swimlanes for each function across the frontstage and backstage lines, as NN/g recommends one blueprint per scenario ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/)). Atlassian's repeated "make the complex simple" suggests the diagram is itself evidence: a slide that tames a messy ecosystem shows the very skill being hired. The common advice to keep the hub to about six to eight spokes is judgement, not a researched limit.

## Bp Premier is a backbone platform under three kinds of system stress

Bp Premier has the same shape as Montu admin, at national scale and with regulators in the loop. Inside a practice it is the shared work surface for three roles. Reception runs appointments, invoicing, claiming and payment terminals. Clinicians use it for the record, prescribing, pathology and imaging requests, and the results inbox. Practice managers handle configuration, MyMedicare site setup, reporting and approval of third-party integrations ([Bp KB: Payment Integrations](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/bppremier/spectra/PaymentIntegrations/PaymentIntegrations.htm); [Bp KB: Request pathology](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/oxford/Clinical/PathologyandRadiology/RequestPathology.htm); [Bp KB: Partner Network](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/Orchid/Management/PartnerNetwork.htm)). A single regulatory field crosses all three. The **MyMedicare registration flag** added in January 2024 shows in the appointment book, on invoices and in the clinical demographics, and a per-location Site ID drives the right incentive items onto the invoice ([Bp KB: Orchid SP1 R1](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/Orchid/WhatsNew/BpPremier_OrchidSP1R1_ReleaseNotes.htm)). A change to that flag is a cross-role change, not a billing feature.

Outward, Bp Premier is the practice's conduit to national infrastructure. That covers Medicare claiming through HICAPS, Tyro or CommBank Smart Health terminals; My Health Record, where Bp Premier 1.12.1 sits on ADHA's January 2026 conformance register; electronic prescribing via the My Script List; and the Australian Immunisation Register and cancer screening register through dedicated utilities ([Bp KB: Payment Integrations](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/bppremier/spectra/PaymentIntegrations/PaymentIntegrations.htm); [ADHA conformance register](https://www.digitalhealth.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/mhr-b2b-conformance-register-20260107.pdf); [Bp KB: What's new (Oxford)](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/bppremier/oxford/WhatsNew/WhatsNewBpPrem.htm)). These edges are regulated. Systems that connect to My Health Record must meet ADHA conformance profiles framed around clinical safety, privacy and security, and ADHA has added a mandatory security conformance profile aligned to the Essential Eight ([ADHA Conformance Profile v1.6](https://implementer.digitalhealth.gov.au/resources/my-health-record-connecting-systems-conformance-profile-v1-6); [ADHA media release](https://digitalhealth.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/media-release---increased-cyber-security-requirements-for-systems-connecting-to-my-health-record_0.pdf)). The national direction is FHIR, through the Sparked AU Core programme ([Sparked](https://sparked.csiro.au/?p=90)). In clinical software, then, systems thinking includes a health-specific second-order effect: a platform change can trigger re-conformance or clinical-safety risk downstream.

Sideways, partners now reach Bp through one governed gateway. **Halo Connect**, a FHIR/REST layer that Bp invested in, became the required route for partners connecting to Bp Premier around the end of 2025 (sources give December 2025 and 1 January 2026). Practices must tick a separate "via Halo Connect" entry, or integrations such as HotDoc stop reading and writing ([Halo Connect FAQ](https://haloconnect.io/faq); [HotDoc support](https://support.hotdoc.com.au/hc/en-gb/articles/16100398145295); [The PHN](https://thephn.com.au/news/halo-connect-and-best-practice-action-required-before-31-december-2025)). The Partner Network list names about **61 partners**, covering booking, payments, AI scribes, analytics, telehealth, PHNs and state health ([Bp KB: Partner Network](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/Orchid/Management/PartnerNetwork.htm)). Bp's own pages claim 130+ or 200+, so counts depend on what is included ([Bp Premier](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/products/bp-premier/)). Bp also both partners with and competes against ecosystem players. Its Best Health Booking sits beside HotDoc and HealthEngine, and its free Lyrebird scribe tier, launched February 2026, sits beside the listed partner Heidi ([Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-launches-best-health-booking-online-booking-management-solution/); [Pulse+IT](https://www.pulseit.news/australian-digital-health/best-practice-launches-free-lyrebird-ai-scribe-tier/)). That tension is classic platform strategy.

The recent friction shows failure spreading along all three axes:

| Axis | Event | How it propagated |
|---|---|---|
| Policy into release | Assignment of Benefit reform, June 2026 | Government changed the rules on 18 June, about eight working days before the 1 July start. Bp pulled its Oxford build, and roughly **1,500 clinics** already on it needed a July update to stop AoB workflows auto-activating. Bp put the project cost at about **A$1m** ([Medical Republic](https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/?p=126623); [Bp support notice](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/support/AoBchanges.htm)) |
| Integration route change | Halo Connect mandate, end 2025 to March 2026 | Every partner had to migrate and every practice had to opt in. HealthEngine set a 31 March 2026 cut-off ([HealthEngine](https://support.healthengine.com.au/en-au/help-centre-practice/enabling-healthengine-as-third-party-integration-for-best-practice-via-halo-connect-1)) |
| Security hardening | Oxford SP1 Database Connector, August 2026 | Unique per-practice database passwords and admin approval of workstations. Third-party firewalls may need manual rules, and some sites lost server–client and Halo Link connections ([Bp KB: What's new](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/bppremier/oxford/WhatsNew/WhatsNewBpPrem.htm); [Bp KB known issues](https://kb.bestpracticesoftware.com/bppremier/oxford/WhatsNew/AllKnownIssues.htm)) |

Smaller dependencies compound these. Releases carry data-update prerequisites. Bp's own release notes tell practices to confirm their third-party integrations still work after upgrading. And the March 2025 shutdown of Argus secure messaging forced practices to switch provider ([Bp downloads](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/bp-prem-downloads/); [Bp KB: Saffron release notes](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/saffron/InstallAndUpgrade/ReleaseNotes/BpPremier_Saffron_ReleaseNotes.htm); [Bp KB: Argus](https://kb.bpsoftware.net/bppremier/Orchid/Integrations/Argus/InstallingArgus.htm)). Third-party CVE aggregators report a hardcoded shared SQL Server credential, CVE-2026-114119, fixed in build 1.14.1.1128 ([strix.ai](https://www.strix.ai/cve/CVE-2026-114119)). No Bp or official advisory confirming it was found, so do not raise it in an interview as fact. The plausible link between that credential issue and the push to Halo Connect is likewise an inference, not something Bp has stated.

## The Montu-to-Bp mapping is close enough to argue directly

The parallels hold at the level a Head of Product cares about. Both platforms serve several internal roles with conflicting jobs. Both sit between regulated external systems and the people doing the work. Both turn one data-quality failure into work for teams further down the chain. The table below sets out the mapping a candidate can make out loud.

| Systems concept | Montu admin (candidate's evidence) | Bp Premier (the role's problem) |
|---|---|---|
| Internal teams as customers | Patient support, fulfilment, pharmacy ops, warehouse, finance | Reception, clinicians, practice managers |
| Regulated edges | Prescription and dispensing compliance | Medicare claiming, My Health Record conformance, ePrescribing, AIR |
| One field, many roles | Order or script status driving dispatch, support and reconciliation | MyMedicare flag driving appointments, invoices and incentive items |
| Partners and hand-offs | Pharmacy, logistics and payment providers | ~60 partners via Halo Connect; payment terminals; messaging vendors |
| Change that cascades | Validation or status change rippling to five teams | AoB release, credential hardening, integration route change |
| Ecosystem metric | Rework, tickets, time to dispatch, reconciliation effort | Release stability, integration uptime, partner migration, adoption of opt-in cloud modules |

Bp's hiring signals point the same way. Recent ads included a Product Delivery Leader tasked with shipping "safely, reliably, and on time" and a Product Leader owning how the company builds and ships software ([Employment Hero](https://employmenthero.com/jobs/position/best-practice-software-pty-ltd-product-delivery-leader-abgbx/); [Employment Hero](https://employmenthero.com/jobs/position/best-practice-software-pty-ltd-product-leader-kqjmx/)). Product reports into a CTO whose remit spans "product, platform, partnerships and technology" ([Bp Leadership](https://bestpracticesoftware.com/about/leadership/)). That combined remit is itself a systems view of the job. A candidate presenting to this audience should mark the regulated edges on the hub diagram and name the standard on each. They should frame rollout as change management for practices with uneven IT maturity. And they should describe one decision in the language of reversibility, because Oxford SP1 made AoB impossible to disable once enabled, a one-way door shipped to thousands of sites.

Steelman the opposing view. A Head of Product at Bp could reasonably say that a cannabis telehealth admin tool has no Medicare claiming, no My Health Record conformance and no on-premise SQL Server estate, and that a five-team internal tool is a long way from 7,000 practices. The answer is not to overclaim domain knowledge. It is to show that the *method* transfers (map actors, blueprint one scenario, model the cascade, pick the high-leverage intervention, measure ecosystem health) and that the candidate has already done the homework on Bp's specific edges. What remains genuinely unknown is how Bp's product team itself assesses systems thinking. No public statement from its leadership was found, and the brief's suggested partner counts and timelines conflict across Bp's own pages.

## Conclusion

The real shift for this candidate is from defending an internal tool as "not customer-facing" to presenting it as the most senior kind of PM work there is. That work is judged by how other teams perform, constrained by regulation, and exposed to second-order failure. Bp's 2026 has been a year of exactly those failures. Policy volatility, a forced partner migration and security hardening all landed on a legacy on-premise base. That makes "I have run a backbone that five teams depend on, and here is how one change cascaded" the most relevant sentence a candidate can bring.

The sharpest differentiator is leverage, not mapping. Many candidates can draw an ecosystem. Fewer can say which intervention sat high on Meadows' ladder, such as an information-flow or rule change rather than another field, and what they consciously left unoptimised. For Bp specifically, the open questions worth taking into the room are these: how product measures release safety across a hybrid on-premise and cloud estate; how it balances partnering with competing inside its own ecosystem; and who owns the adoption story for opt-in cloud modules, which nobody yet publishes numbers for.
